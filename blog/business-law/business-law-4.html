1) Mini Underwriting Model (Mapped to a PPM)

Let’s simulate a simple deal straight out of a Private Placement Memorandum.

🏢 Deal Overview (from PPM)

  • Purchase Price: $20M
  • Units: 100
  • Avg Rent: $1,200/month
  • Vacancy: 8%
  • Expense Ratio: 45%
  • Loan: 65% LTV, 6.5% interest
  • Hold: 5 years
  • Exit Cap: 6.5%

Step 1: Revenue (Top Line)

Gross Potential Rent (GPR):

  • 100 units × 1,200×12=∗∗1,200 × 12 = **1,200×12=∗∗1,440,000**

Effective Gross Income (EGI):

  • Adjust for vacancy (8%):
  • 1.44M×(1−0.08)=∗∗1.44M × (1 - 0.08) = **1.44M×(1−0.08)=∗∗1,324,800**

Step 2: Expenses

  • Expenses = 45% of EGI
  • 1,324,800×0.45=∗∗1,324,800 × 0.45 = **1,324,800×0.45=∗∗596,160**

Step 3: NOI (Key Metric)

  • NOI = EGI - Expenses
  • NOI = $728,640

This is the most important number in CRE underwriting.

Step 4: Debt Service

Loan:

  • 65% of 20M=20M = 20M=13M

Interest-only (simplified):

  • 13M×6.513M × 6.5% = **13M×6.5845,000**

Step 5: Cash Flow

  • Cash Flow = NOI - Debt Service
  • = 728,640−728,640 - 728,640−845,000
  • = -$116,360

🚩 Immediate red flag:

Negative cash flow → risky unless strong value-add

Step 6: Exit Value

Using exit cap rate:

We apply the core valuation relationship:

  • Exit NOI (assume growth to $900K)
  • Value = 900K/6.5900K / 6.5% = **~900K/6.513.85M**

  • Bought at 20M→sellingat20M → selling at 20M→sellingat13.85M = value destruction

Step 7: Investor Returns

Equity invested:

  • $7M

If exit value is lower:

  • Investors likely lose money

💡 What an Underwriter Says:

“This deal only works if NOI grows aggressively. Otherwise, it's underwater.”

2) Interview-Ready Underwriting Checklist

This is what you should literally memorize for interviews.

🔍 The “PPM Breakdown Framework”

1. Deal Overview

  • What is being acquired?
  • What’s the business plan?

2. Revenue Assumptions

  • Rent vs market comps
  • Vacancy vs market

Interview line:

“I always benchmark rents and vacancy against market data.”

3. Expense Assumptions

  • % of revenue
  • Any underestimation?

Red flag:

  • Expenses < 35% in multifamily (usually unrealistic)

4. NOI Quality

  • Is NOI stable or dependent on growth?

Key insight:

“I focus on in-place NOI vs pro forma NOI.”

5. Capital Stack

  • Debt terms (LTV, rate, structure)
  • Equity contribution

Red flag:

  • High leverage + floating rate debt

6. Debt Coverage

Rule of thumb:

  • DSCR < 1.25 = risky

7. Exit Assumptions

  • Exit cap vs market cap
  • Too optimistic?

Interview line:

“I stress exit cap rates upward to test downside.”

8. Returns

  • IRR vs risk level
  • Driven by operations or exit?

Red flag:

  • Returns mostly from exit multiple expansion

9. Sponsor Quality

  • Track record
  • Co-invest

Interview line:

“I weigh sponsor experience as heavily as the numbers.”

10. Downside Case

  • What breaks the deal?

Interview killer answer:

“I always ask: what assumptions need to be true for this deal to work—and what happens if they’re not?”

3) How to Sound Elite in Interviews

If they ask: “How do you analyze a deal?”

You can say:

“I start by breaking down the PPM into revenue, expenses, and NOI to understand in-place performance. Then I rebuild the model with more conservative assumptions, stress key variables like rent growth and exit cap rates, and evaluate DSCR and downside protection. Finally, I assess whether returns are driven by operational improvements or market appreciation, and whether the sponsor has a strong track record and alignment of incentives.”
