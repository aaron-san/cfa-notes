## Business Law

In finance, **PPM** usually stands for **Private Placement Memorandum**.

### What a PPM is

A PPM is a **legal disclosure document** used when a company raises money from private investors (instead of going public). It's common in:

- Private equity deals
- Real estate syndications
- Hedge funds
- Venture capital raises

### What it does

Think of it as a **detailed pitch + risk disclosure**. Its main purposes:

- Explain the investment opportunity
- Disclose all **risks** (very important legally)
- Protect the issuer from liability

### What's inside a PPM

Typical sections include:

- **Executive summary** – overview of the deal
- **Investment strategy** – how money will be used
- **Risk factors** – market risk, liquidity risk, etc.
- **Financial projections**
- **Management team background**
- **Legal terms** – fees, structure, investor rights

### Example (real estate context)

If a sponsor is raising money for an apartment deal:

- The PPM explains the property, expected returns, and risks
- Investors review it before committing capital

---

### Other meanings (less common in finance)

PPM can also mean:

- **Parts per million** (used in economics/data, not really finance-specific)
- **Project Portfolio Management** (more corporate/operations context)

## Private Placement Memorandum

A Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) is a legal document provided to potential investors in a private securities offering. It outlines details of the investment, associated risks, management background, and legal disclosures. The PPM is central to ensuring compliance with securities laws and protecting both issuers and investors in non-public capital raises.

### Key facts

- **Purpose:** Disclosure document for private securities offerings
- **Used by:** Companies raising capital privately under exemptions (e.g., Regulation D in the U.S.)
- **Includes:** Risk factors, financial statements, use of proceeds, management bios
- **Legal role:** Demonstrates regulatory compliance and mitigates liability
- **Audience:** Accredited or institutional investors

### Structure and Content

A typical PPM contains sections describing the issuer’s business, terms of the offering, and the securities being sold. It includes audited financial statements, descriptions of key risks, capitalization structure, and management experience. Additional sections may detail conflicts of interest, investor suitability standards, and subscription procedures.

### Regulatory Context

PPMs are used primarily in exempt offerings under U.S. securities laws, such as **Regulation D**. These exemptions allow issuers to raise capital without full registration with the **U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission**, provided they meet specific disclosure and investor qualification requirements. While not always mandated, a comprehensive PPM helps demonstrate compliance and good faith disclosure.

### Importance and Investor Protection

The document serves as a risk management tool. For issuers, it limits exposure to fraud or misrepresentation claims. For investors, it offers transparency regarding financial performance, governance, and potential downsides, supporting informed decision-making in illiquid, higher-risk private investments.

### Use in Practice

PPMs are standard in venture capital, private equity, and hedge fund fundraising. They act as both marketing and legal disclosure documents—often supplemented by a term sheet and subscription agreement—to formalize the relationship between investors and issuers before capital commitments are made.
