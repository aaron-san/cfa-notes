Execution of Portfolio Decisions

Market Order

  • Instruction to execute an order promptly at the best price available
  • Transaction certainty
  • Price uncertainty

Limit Order

  • Instruction to trade at or better than the limit price specified in the order
  • Price certainty
  • Transaction uncertainty

Quote-Driven (Dealer) Markets

  • Investors trade with dealers (market makers) who post prices at which securities can be bought and sold
  • Dealers maintain inventories of securities:
    • Stand ready to buy and place into inventory or sell out of inventory
    • Earn the bid-ask spread for providing liquidity

Order-Driven Markets

  • Investors post limit orders, traders transact with other traders (not with intermediaries)
  • Liquidity in the market is determined by the number of traders/orders
  • Orders mechanically matched by a pre-specified set of rules
    • Trader does not choose (may not even know) the other side of the trade
  • Types of Order-Driven Markets
  1. Electronic Crossing Networks (ECNs)
  • Orders are batched together and crossed (matched) at fixed points in time during the day at the average of the bid and ask quotes.
  • Maintains trader anonymity
  • Avoid dealer costs
  • Market/Price impact benefits Decimalization in the U.S. has led to dramatic reductions in spreads.
  • Often used by institutions
  • Trade prices are determined by external markets
  1. Auction markets (periodic/batch or continuous)
  • Traders post their orders and all orders compete for execution
  1. Automated auctions (electronic limit-order markets)
  • Computerized auction markets provide price discovery
  • There is a progression toward order-driven systems. FIX - messaging protocol in equity markets that facilitates electronic trading

Brokered Markets

  • Markets in which brokers act as traders' agents to find counterparties
    • Used for large block trades, when anonymity is desired, and/or the market for the security is small or illiquid.
  • A block trade entails a quantity that is large relative to the liquidity ordinarily available from dealers in the security or in other markets

Hybrid Markets

  • Combination of quote-driven, order-driven, and broker markets
  • NYSE is a good example - offers elements of batch auction markets (e.g., the opening) and continuous auction markets (intraday trading), as well as quote-driven markets (the role of the specialists)
    • Batch market: all trades settled at the same price

Broker is an agent of the investor (trader) who in return for a commission performs a variety of execution services, including:

  • Representing the order to the market
  • Finding the opposite of a trade if interest is not currently evident in the market
  • Supplying market information
  • Providing annonymity and confidentiality
  • Providing margin financing
  • Supporting the market mechanism - brokerage commissions indirectly assure the continuance of the needed market - acilities -

Execution Costs

  • Used to measure best execution (actual transaction costs - - s. benchmark)
  1. Explicit costs ("CTSF")
  • Commissions from brokerage ▪Taxes ▪Stamp duties ▪Fees paid to exchange
  1. Implicit costs: indirect trading costs: ("MLBD") ▪Bid-Ask spread ▪Market (or price) Impact - effect of the trade on prices ▪Lost opportunity cost - missed profit from failure to execute a trade

  • Delay costs (or slippage) - arise from the inability to complete the desired trade immediately due to its size and the liquidity of markets
