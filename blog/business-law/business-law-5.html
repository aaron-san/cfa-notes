1) Excel-Style Underwriting Template (Step-by-Step)

This mirrors how analysts rebuild a deal from a Private Placement Memorandum.

You can set this up in Excel exactly as shown.

🧱 Sheet 1: Assumptions (Inputs)

Purchase Price:        20,000,000
Units:                 100
Avg Rent / Month:      1,200
Vacancy Rate:          8%
Other Income %:        5%
Expense Ratio:         45%

Rent Growth:           3%
Expense Growth:        2.5%

Loan LTV:              65%
Interest Rate:         6.5%
Amortization:          30 years

Hold Period:           5
Exit Cap Rate:         6.75%
Selling Costs:         2%

📊 Sheet 2: Operating Model (Year 1 Example)

Revenue

Gross Potential Rent = Units * Rent * 12
= 100 * 1,200 * 12 = 1,440,000

Other Income = 5% * GPR = 72,000

Effective Gross Income (EGI):
= (GPR + Other Income) * (1 - Vacancy)
= (1,440,000 + 72,000) * 0.92 = 1,389,120

Expenses

Expenses = 45% * EGI
= 625,104

NOI

NOI = EGI - Expenses
= 764,016

💰 Sheet 3: Debt Model

Loan Amount:

= Purchase Price * LTV = 13,000,000

Annual Debt Service (use mortgage formula in Excel):

=PMT(rate, periods, loan amount)

= PMT(6.5%/12, 360, -13,000,000) * 12

📉 Sheet 4: Cash Flow

Cash Flow Before Tax = NOI - Debt Service

Pro Tip (What real analysts do)

Don't trust the PPM:

  • Build Base Case
  • Build Downside Case
  • Build Upside Case

Example:

  • Rent growth: 3% → test 1%
  • Exit cap: 6.75% → test 7.75%

2. Technical Interview Questions + Answers (CRE / Credit / IB)

These are exactly what you'll get asked.

🔹 Q1: Walk me through how you underwrite a deal

"I start with revenue by analyzing rents, vacancy, and other income, then estimate expenses to derive NOI. From there I layer in the capital structure, calculate debt service and DSCR, and project cash flows over the hold period. I then estimate exit value using a cap rate and calculate IRR and equity multiple. Finally, I stress key assumptions like rent growth and exit cap rate to evaluate downside risk."

🔹 Q2: What is NOI and why does it matter?

"NOI, or Net Operating Income, is revenue minus operating expenses before debt. It's critical because it determines both cash flow and property value through capitalization."

🔹 Q3: How do you value a property?

"Primarily using the income approach by capitalizing NOI."

"It measures the property's ability to cover debt payments. Lenders typically want at least 1.2-1.3x."

🔹 Q5: What are the biggest risks in a deal?

  • Rent overestimation
  • Expense underestimation
  • Interest rate increases
  • Exit cap expansion

👉 Strong closer:

"Most deals fail due to overly optimistic assumptions rather than execution."

🔹 Q6: What drives IRR?

  • NOI growth
  • Leverage
  • Exit price

👉 Bonus:

"IRR is very sensitive to exit assumptions."

🔹 Q7: What's a red flag in a PPM?

  • Aggressive rent growth (5%+)
  • Low expense ratio
  • Exit cap lower than market
  • No sponsor co-invest

🔹 Q8: Difference between in-place vs pro forma?

"In-place reflects current performance, while pro forma reflects projected performance after improvements. I always anchor to in-place to avoid overpaying."

🔹 Q9: How do interest rates affect value?

"Higher rates increase cap rates and reduce property values, while also increasing debt costs and lowering cash flow."

🔹 Q10: What's more important—IRR or equity multiple?

"IRR measures time-adjusted returns, while equity multiple shows total profit. I look at both depending on investment horizon."
