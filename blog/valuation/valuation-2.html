  • Growth must be paid for with regular reinvestments

Relative Valuation

  • Most assets are valued on a relative basis
  • Valuation is determined by the pricing of similar assets using a common variable
  • Relative valuation is appropriate for analysts looking for undervalued assets in any market no matter how overvalued and who are judged by relative performance
  • Relatively cheap stocks can always be found even in overvalued markets
  • Standardized ratios (multiples) are used to compare similar assets
  • Multiples often use earnings, book value, revenues, cash flows as the common variable
  • Multiples can be based on equity or firm (enterprise) value
  • Intrinsic value is not estimated with relative valuation
  • Relative valuation assumes markets, on average are correctly priced

Variations on Relative valuation

  1. Direct comparison - compare the market price of the target firm with the market price of one or more similar firms
  2. Peer group average - compare the target firm's multiple to the average multiple of a peer group of similar firms (assumes the market as a whole is representative for a typical company)
  3. Peer group average adjusted for differences - can use statistical methods (e.g., multiple regression) to adjust for differences between the target firm and the peer group (allows us to expand the comparable firm sample to the entire market)

Relative Valuation - Key Points

  • Multiples are easy to compute and understand
  • Multiples are easy to misuse and manipulate
  • When comparing multiples across companies, it is important to ensure that the companies are comparable in terms of risk, cash flows, and growth prospects
  • The definition of a comparable firm is a subjective decision
  • Assumptions are often left unstated, unlike in DCF valuation

Contingent Claim Valuation

  • Used to value assets with option-like characteristics
  • Appropriate when there is a significant exclusive benefit obtained from learning and flexibility in decision making
  • Examples include employee stock options, warrants, convertibles, and real options
  • Uses option pricing models such as Black-Scholes, binomial, or Monte Carlo simulation
  • Example projects to value:
    • Patents awaiting the approval process by regulatory bodies
    • Natural resource extraction projects (e.g., oil reserves, gold mines)
    • Research and development projects
    • Abandonment options
    • Expansion options
    • Equity in an unprofitable company with significant debt
